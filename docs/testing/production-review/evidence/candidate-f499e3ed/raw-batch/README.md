# Real RAW workload stopped on placement coverage

The first f499e3ed series failed its all-worker check on the second measured mesh batch. The public CLI itself exited 0 after 9.518 seconds. No valid three-pair median or speedup follows from this partial run. The frozen benchmark, its failure log and the first-series data are retained. The two warmups and three measured batches before the failed arm fully validated both real photographs. Native decode, tile accounting, reopened linear RGB16 PNGs and complete output-byte verification succeeded. Their Canon and Nikon PNG SHA-256s matched the earlier fixed reference. The signed plugin processed 12 Canon tiles and 6 Nikon tiles per full batch. The mesh warmup distributed 8, 6 and 4 tiles among its three registered peers. The first measured mesh arm distributed 8, 5 and 5 tiles and the paired single-worker arm handled all 18. Both original RAW hashes were independently rechecked after the failed series. [Photo provenance](../../../../raw-image-fixtures.md)

In the failing arm the verifier had already checked a CLI summary of 2 processed, 0 resumed and 0 failed; success status, known unique filename, allowed and numerically consistent worker tile receipts and PNG decode for every emitted record. The exact CLI increments its count and prints one JSON record for each processed photo; propagation of output errors precedes the final successful summary and exit. With two known inputs and the observed successful summary, the remaining joint coverage predicate implicates one or more of the three specified peers receiving zero tiles. The driver captured the failing arm's wall time and CLI status but persisted neither its stdout nor the derived per-photo records before raising. It also compares output hashes with the earlier reference only after its coverage predicate. The identity of the omitted peer and final two output hashes for this arm are unobservable.

The coordinator sent the same three-peer allowlist for each of the 18 tiles, with three outstanding tile jobs at most. The coordinator has no local plugin. The scheduler filters for currently usable peers and uses measured RTT, transport size/throughput, cost and pending load to choose among eligible workers. An allowlist authorizes candidates; it does not require a minimum number of tasks per peer. The benchmark independently requires complete worker participation before it will grade mesh speedup. This outcome establishes a benchmark gate failure without evidence of an unauthorized worker or failure to render a camera input. The frozen RAW comparison did not retain per-tile score inputs, so specific score or eligibility changes cannot be assigned as the cause.
